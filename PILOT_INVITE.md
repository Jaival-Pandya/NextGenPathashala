# How to invite an institution to test NextGenPathashala

Use the message that feels natural for the person you are contacting. The goal of the first conversation is not to pressure someone into replacing their existing software. It is to discover whether the product can make one daily task easier.

## 30-second introduction

"I'm building **NextGenPathashala**, an early-stage education workspace for daily class coordination. It brings a class timetable, attendance, homework and notices together in one place. I'm inviting a small number of schools, colleges and tuition centres to test one sample class and tell me honestly what works and what gets in the way. I'm not asking you to replace your current system. Would you be open to a short 10–15 minute test?"

## WhatsApp / direct message

Hi! I'm building **NextGenPathashala**, a simple early-access tool for keeping timetables, attendance, homework and class notices together.

I'm looking for a few school, college and tuition educators to try a sample workspace for 10–15 minutes and share honest feedback. You can test one class, add fictional users, mark attendance, publish homework and check how the student view works.

You don't need to replace your current software or registers. Please use fictional sample data for this early test. I'm most interested in what is genuinely useful, what feels confusing, and what is missing from your daily workflow.

Would you be willing to try it? **[Insert your deployed website URL]**

Thank you—critical feedback is especially helpful at this stage.

## Tailor the introduction to the institution

### School principal or administrator

"I'd like to learn how your team currently handles the daily timetable, class attendance, homework and notices. NextGenPathashala is a small test workspace for those flows. Could you try it with one sample class and tell me what is missing for a school? I know school processes are more complex than a demo, so the goal is to identify gaps before asking anyone to rely on it."

### College administrator or faculty member

"I'm testing whether one simple workspace can make class schedules, attendance, assignments and notices easier to find. Could you try a sample subject or division and tell me which college-specific requirements are missing—such as labs, substitutions, semesters or faculty scheduling?"

### Tuition or coaching owner

"I'd like to see whether a simple batch timetable, attendance register, homework list and notice board would save effort in your daily tuition workflow. Could we try one fictional batch together for 10 minutes? If it doesn't make the task easier, I'd like to know why."

## Five discovery questions to ask before showing the app

1. How do you currently share the timetable and notify people when a class changes?
2. Who marks attendance, and how much time does it usually take for one class?
3. Where do students find homework and class notes today?
4. What is the most frustrating repeated task for teachers or administrators?
5. If a new tool could solve just one of those problems reliably, which one would matter most?

Listen first. Don't assume that the person's largest problem is the one your current MVP addresses.

## A 10–15 minute demonstration

1. **Start with their problem (2 minutes).** Repeat the workflow they said was frustrating and ask whether you understood it correctly.
2. **Create one sample class (2 minutes).** Show how a group/batch and its users are organised.
3. **Schedule one class (2 minutes).** Show the date, subject, teacher and room.
4. **Mark attendance (2 minutes).** Sign in as the teacher and save sample attendance.
5. **Show the student view (2 minutes).** Confirm the student sees their timetable, attendance, homework and target notice.
6. **Ask for critique (3–5 minutes).** Ask them to identify the most confusing step and the one improvement that would make it useful.

Do not spend the full demonstration talking about future AI ideas or features that do not exist yet. Show the current working flow and explain limitations clearly.

## Responding to common objections

**"We already use another system."**  
"That makes sense. I'm not asking you to switch. Could you compare one sample workflow with what you already use and tell me whether there's any gap worth solving? If your current tool already handles it well, that's useful feedback too."

**"Our teachers won't use another app."**  
"That's a real concern. Could one teacher try just the attendance or homework flow? I'll ask them how many steps felt unnecessary and prioritise reducing that friction."

**"Can we use this for our official student records now?"**  
"Not yet. This is an early MVP and needs full runtime, privacy, backup and recovery verification. Please use fictional or explicitly authorised test data and keep official records in your existing system during the pilot."

**"What if something goes wrong?"**  
"I'm still validating the product. We'll agree the scope of a small test, keep your current system as the source of truth, and record problems through the feedback form. I won't promise production-grade reliability before it's demonstrated."

## Follow-up after the test

Thank you for trying NextGenPathashala. Could you share:

- The most useful part, if any.
- The step that was most confusing or slow.
- One workflow that is still missing for your institution.
- Whether you would test it again after that issue is fixed.

Honest negative feedback is valuable. Do not ask for a positive testimonial unless the person has genuinely chosen to recommend the product.

## Pilot safety

- Use fictional or properly authorised test data.
- Do not collect student names, marks, medical details, passwords or other sensitive information through a marketing or feedback form.
- Do not promise features, integrations or reliability that have not been tested.
- Leave the institution's existing official register/software as the source of truth until deployment, security and recovery checks have passed.
